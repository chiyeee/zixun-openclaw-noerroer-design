How to Find Products to Sell on Amazon: 7 Proven Methods

HN

Written by Henk Nie

Updated Mar 26, 2026 • 25 min read

Finding the right product is the hardest part of building an Amazon business. Get it wrong, and you're stuck with inventory that collects dust. Get it right, and you've built the foundation for a six-figure business.

The problem? Most sellers rely on gut feeling or copy whatever's trending on TikTok. That's not product research—that's gambling.

This guide breaks down seven data-driven methods to find products to sell on Amazon. Each method works independently, but the real power comes from combining them. By the end, you'll have a systematic approach to identify high-potential products before your competitors do.

What Makes a Good Product to Sell on Amazon?

Before diving into research methods, you need to know what you're looking for. A profitable Amazon product typically meets five criteria:

Criteria Target Why It Matters
Demand BSR < 50,000 in main category Confirms people are actually buying
Competition < 500 reviews on top 3 sellers Room for new entrants
Profit Margin > 30% after all fees Covers ads, returns, and surprises
Price Range $15 - $50 High enough for margin, low enough for impulse buys
Sourcing Available on Alibaba/1688 You can actually get it made

These aren't arbitrary numbers. According to Jungle Scout's research, products in the $15-50 range with fewer than 500 competing reviews have the highest success rate for new sellers.

Method 1: Start with Amazon Best Sellers

The simplest method is hiding in plain sight: Amazon's own Best Sellers page.

How It Works

  1. Go to Amazon Best Sellers
  2. Browse subcategories (the deeper, the better)
  3. Look for products ranking well but with low review counts
  4. Check "Movers & Shakers" for trending items

What to Look For

  • Products in the #20-100 range with fewer than 200 reviews
  • Items where the top seller doesn't dominate
  • Categories you can realistically source products for
💡 Pro Tip: Focus on subcategories, not main categories. "Kitchen & Dining" is too broad. Deep subcategories are where opportunities hide.

Method 2: Use Amazon Product Opportunity Explorer

Amazon's Product Opportunity Explorer is a free tool that shows you search demand versus existing supply—essentially telling you where customers want products that don't exist yet.

How It Works

  1. Log into Seller Central
  2. Navigate to Growth > Product Opportunity Explorer
  3. Search for a niche or browse categories
  4. Analyze the "Niche Score" (higher = better opportunity)

Key Metrics to Check

  • Search Volume: How many customers are searching for this
  • Search Volume Growth: Is demand increasing or declining
  • Average Units Sold: What the typical seller moves
  • Number of Products: How crowded the niche is

Why It's Powerful: This is first-party Amazon data—not estimates from third-party tools. When Amazon tells you a niche has high demand and low competition, that's as close to insider information as you'll get legally.

Method 3: Reverse Engineer Competitor Products

Instead of starting from scratch, start with products that already sell and find ways to improve them.

How It Works

  1. Find a product with strong sales but mediocre reviews (3.5-4.2 stars)
  2. Read the negative reviews systematically
  3. Identify the top 3-5 complaints
  4. Create a product that solves those problems

Real-World Example

Say you're researching ceramic coffee mugs. You notice the top sellers have complaints like "Handle broke after 2 weeks", "Smaller than pictured", "No lid option". Your opportunity: A ceramic mug with a reinforced handle, accurate sizing photos, and an included lid.

Method 4: Keyword-First Research

Most sellers start with a product idea and then check if people search for it. Flip that approach: start with what people are searching for, then find products to match.

How It Works

  1. Use Amazon's search bar (type a keyword and watch autocomplete)
  2. Find keywords with high search volume but low competition
  3. Analyze the products currently ranking for those keywords
  4. Identify gaps you can fill

Amazon Search Bar Trick

Type a seed keyword like "coffee mug" and let Amazon autocomplete. Those suggestions are real searches from real customers:

  • coffee mug with lid ← specific feature request
  • coffee mug for men ← demographic targeting
  • coffee mug warmer ← accessory opportunity

Method 5: Social Listening (Reddit, TikTok, Forums)

Amazon data tells you what people are buying. Social media tells you what people are frustrated about—before they even search for solutions.

Platform What to Search Signal
Reddit r/BuyItForLife, r/shutupandtakemymoney "I wish someone made..."
TikTok #AmazonFinds, #TikTokMadeMeBuyIt Viral products before they peak
Niche Forums Hobbyist communities Underserved enthusiast products

Method 6: Supplier-First Research (1688/Alibaba)

Most sellers do research backwards: they find a product on Amazon, then hunt for a supplier. Flip it.

How It Works

  1. Browse Alibaba or 1688 (Chinese domestic site, lower prices)
  2. Filter by "Ready to Ship" or high-volume suppliers
  3. Look at what they're already producing at scale
  4. Check if those products have demand on Amazon

Why This Works: Suppliers know what sells. If a factory is mass-producing something, there's demand somewhere. Your job is to connect that supply to the Amazon marketplace.

Method 7: AI-Powered Automation

Each method above works. But doing them manually takes hours—and you're competing against sellers who don't.

The Automation Advantage

  1. Data Collection: Scrape BSR, pricing, and review data automatically
  2. Review Analysis: Extract complaint patterns from thousands of reviews
  3. Keyword Research: Find search terms with favorable competition ratios
  4. Validation: Cross-reference demand signals across platforms

✅ Sourcing 3+ suppliers found Alibaba

✅ Legal No patents/trademarks USPTO

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Chasing Trends Too Late

By the time a product goes viral, hundreds have already placed orders. Look for rising trends, not peaked ones.

Mistake 2: Ignoring Seasonality

A product selling 1,000 units in December might sell 50 in February. Check historical BSR data before committing.

Mistake 3: Underestimating Competition

"Only 200 reviews" looks easy until you realize those reviews are from a brand with a $50,000/month ad budget.

Mistake 4: Skipping the Math

A $25 product with $8 costs sounds great—until you add FBA fees ($5), PPC ($3), returns ($1). You're making $8 per unit, not $17.

Conclusion

Finding products to sell on Amazon isn't about luck—it's about process. The seven methods covered here give you multiple angles to identify opportunities. The sellers who win aren't smarter—they're more systematic. Pick a method, validate your findings, and take action before the opportunity closes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does Amazon product research take? ▼
Thorough product research typically takes 2-4 weeks for beginners. With automation tools like OpenClaw, you can reduce this to 3-5 days.
What is the best price range for Amazon FBA products? ▼
The sweet spot is $15-50. The $20-35 range offers the best balance of margin and conversion rate.
How do I know if a niche is too competitive? ▼
Too competitive if: top 10 have 1,000+ reviews, major brands dominate, PPC costs exceed $2/click, or category requires brand approval.
Can I do Amazon product research for free? ▼
Yes. Free methods: Best Sellers, search autocomplete, Product Opportunity Explorer, Google Trends, Reddit, Alibaba supplier browsing.
What BSR is good for Amazon products? ▼
BSR under 50,000 indicates consistent sales. BSR 10,000-30,000 is the sweet spot—enough demand but not too competitive.
How many products should I research before choosing? ▼
Research 20-30 products. Maybe 5-10 pass initial screening. After deeper analysis, pick the one with clearest competitive advantage.
